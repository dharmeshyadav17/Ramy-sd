About

Two counters, one devotee's kitchen

Maa Vaishno began as a single kerosene stove outside the Maniyar Bari lane, frying four bhature a morning for labourers heading to the fields. Three decades later the same dough recipe — rested overnight, fried in wood-pressed mustard oil — is served alongside Satyam Sweets, the mithai counter run by the next generation of the family.

When the family moved to a plant-based diet, they converted the whole kitchen rather than split it. Ghee became cold-pressed oil, milk became coconut and cashew, and the chole only got deeper in flavour. Today it is a fully vegan house where every regular — vegan or not — simply calls it "the bhature wala".

Satyam Sharma

Owner

"I learned the dough from my mother. I learned the oil from my conscience."

Satyam Sharma took over the counter from his mother Rukmini in 2011 and quietly rebuilt every recipe from scratch without dairy — a two-year experiment that cost him half his regulars and won him twice as many. He still arrives at 5 AM to punch down the bhature dough and tastes every batch of rabri before the shutter goes up.

"Anyone can make sweets with ghee. Making them taste like home without it — that is the real recipe."
